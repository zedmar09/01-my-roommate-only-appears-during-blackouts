# Page Composition Rules

## Core Principle

The **page is the storytelling unit**. Panel count and geometry are determined by the scene, not by a template.

## Page Canvas

Use one approved source-page specification once chosen for publication. Do not change the canonical page canvas randomly from scene to scene. Visual variety comes from panel composition. A separate long stitched preview/export may combine pages later.

## Valid Page Modes

### Dialogue / Investigation
Typically 5–9 panels. Use medium shots, close-ups, inserts, reaction cuts, and small environment reminders.

### Dramatic / Emotional
Typically 2–5 panels. Give important expressions or gestures more space.

### Reveal
Small setup panels may lead to one dominant half-page or full-page reveal.

### Action / Panic
Use dynamic panel boundaries, strong diagonals, narrow inserts, speed lines, and abrupt scale changes while keeping reading order clear.

### Comedy / Reactions
Many compact panels are allowed. Simplified faces/body language may replace detailed backgrounds.

### Quiet / Atmosphere
Few panels, generous white space inside the page design, environmental detail, and controlled pacing.

### Full Page / Splash
Allowed only when the story earns it. Do not waste splash pages on routine exposition.

## Reading Flow

The final manga reading direction will be locked before production. Until then, page maps must explicitly state intended panel order so generation does not rely on ambiguous geometry.

## Page-Turn Rule

Whenever possible, reserve major entrances, supernatural reveals, reversals, or visual shocks for a page turn rather than spoiling them in the previous page's lower panels.
