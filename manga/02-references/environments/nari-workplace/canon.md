## Workplace Identity

- mid-sized publishing company in Seoul
- ordinary professional editorial office
- neither tiny indie room nor giant luxury corporate headquarters
- grounded contemporary environment
- no game-studio, software-lab, cyberpunk, startup-campus, broadcast newsroom, or detective-office styling

## Nari's Function In This Space

Nari is an editor. Her desk/work environment should naturally support:

- manuscript reading
- printed/digital proofs
- correction notes
- reference books
- page review
- coordination with coworkers/authors
- ordinary calls/messages
- deadline-driven late work

The environment should visually communicate publishing/editorial work without requiring readable brand names, manuscript titles, author names, or logos.

## Recurring Zones

### 1. Editorial Open Work Area

- Nari's recurring desk
- nearby coworker desks
- ordinary computers/monitors as tools, not the visual identity of the job
- manuscripts/proofs/notebooks/office supplies
- modest books/reference shelves
- printer/copier or ordinary publishing-office equipment only where useful

Nari's desk location and orientation must remain stable across views.

### 2. Small Editorial Review / Meeting Area

- modest table / chairs or small enclosed/semi-enclosed meeting room
- supports manuscript/proof discussions and ordinary coworker scenes
- not a high-tech presentation room

### 3. Late-Night Office State

Same geometry as daytime.

- fewer coworkers
- quieter lighting/readability
- Nari can visibly remain at her desk after most staff leave
- no cinematic horror transformation
- the Chapter 001 opening uses this environment to prove Nari is away from home at the time of the apartment noise

## Visual Atmosphere

- practical
- adult
- mildly busy during day
- papers/books/proofs provide texture without chaotic clutter
- believable deadline culture without making every desk a mess
- ordinary contemporary furniture

## Chapter 001 Continuity

The cold open must establish Nari at this office clearly enough that later the reader accepts she was not home when the neighbor directly below heard activity.

Possible visual time anchors may include:

- coworkers leaving
- wall clock shape without requiring readable exact text if unnecessary
- office window night exterior
- proof/manuscript progress
- ordinary message/file context in page production

The environment reference itself does not need to encode story timestamps.

## Manga Rendering

Use the approved series manga style reference WebP once available.

Requirements:

- black-and-white ink
- manga screentone/hatching
- clear perspective
- detailed enough for establishing shots
- simplifiable for dialogue/reaction pages
- no glossy reflective office surfaces
- no cinematic bloom

## Automatic Reject

Reject a generated PNG candidate if:

- office becomes a software/game/engineering workspace
- screens dominate every view and manuscripts/books disappear
- Nari's desk moves between views
- review area geometry changes
- daytime and late-night versions redesign the room
- fake readable publishing logos/titles appear prominently
- environment becomes luxury/futuristic/cyberpunk
- rendering is color, glossy, photoreal, CGI/3D, painterly, or cinematic concept art

Only after the PNG passes this gate should it be manually converted to the final WebP authority.
